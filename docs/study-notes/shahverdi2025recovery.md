# Decision support for prioritizing critical societal services in optimal post-disaster critical lifeline recovery

## Method and findings

Rolling-horizon two-stage stochastic programs; progressive hedging; scenario reduction and parallelization. Synthetic geographically grounded network, 182 components/two hospitals; objective-weight and scenario-size experiments.

## Critical appraisal and project relevance

One-increment repairs, binary states, hospitals assumed undamaged and functional once lifelines available, trivial travel durations; time increments are not universal hours. Scheduling given objectives differs from deriving business tolerance. Results cannot be transferred as project effectiveness percentages.

Project implications are review interpretations, not validated project results.
